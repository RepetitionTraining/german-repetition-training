Adjektivdeklination#

Adjective endings, nominative, accusative, and dative

See also

  • A2 > Grammar > Komparativ und Superlativ

  • A2 > Grammar > Akkusativ – Grundlagen

  • A2 > Grammar > Dativ – Grundlagen

  • A2 > Grammar > Pronomen: alles, etwas, nichts

In German, adjectives that appear directly before a noun change their ending to match the noun’s gender, the case, and the type of article before them. This page covers the three cases you use most: the Nominativ (subject), the Akkusativ (direct object), and the Dativ (indirect object and after common prepositions).

Note

Adjectives that follow a verb like sein, werden, or bleiben do not change:

  • Das Wetter ist schön. – The weather is nice.

  • Der Kaffee wird kalt. – The coffee is getting cold.

Only adjectives that sit directly before a noun take an ending.

Important

The ending depends on three things:

  1. Gender of the noun, masculine (der), feminine (die), neuter (das), or plural

  2. Case, what grammatical role the noun plays in the sentence

  3. Article type, definite (der/die/das), indefinite or possessive (ein/mein/kein …), or no article

Tip

After a definite article, the singular ending is always -e, never -er or -es. The article already shows which gender the noun is, so the adjective only needs a light touch.

Examples#

  • Der neue Zug kommt pünktlich an.
    The new train arrives on time. (masc, definite)

  • Eine kleine Katze schläft auf dem Sofa.
    A small cat is sleeping on the sofa. (fem, indefinite)

  • Das große Fenster gibt viel Licht.
    The large window lets in a lot of light. (neut, definite)

  • Ein kalter Wind kommt vom Berg.
    A cold wind comes from the mountain. (masc, indefinite, note -er)

  • Frisches Brot riecht wunderbar.
    Fresh bread smells wonderful. (neut, no article, note -es)

  • Die kleinen Kinder spielen draußen.
    The small children are playing outside. (plural, definite)

Tip

Only masculine changes between Nominativ and Akkusativ, it always takes -en in the accusative, regardless of the article type. Feminine, neuter, and plural are identical to their Nominativ forms.

Examples#

  • Ich kaufe den alten Pulli.
    I’m buying the old sweater. (masc, definite)

  • Er bestellt einen großen Kaffee.
    He orders a large coffee. (masc, indefinite, note -en, not -er)

  • Wir besuchen die alte Kirche.
    We visit the old church. (fem, definite, same ending as Nominativ)

  • Hast du ein gutes Buch für mich?
    Do you have a good book for me? (neut, indefinite, same ending as Nominativ)

  • Ich mag frischen Orangensaft.
    I like fresh orange juice. (masc, no article, note -en)

  • Siehst du den kleinen Hund dort drüben?
    Do you see the small dog over there? (masc, definite)

Dativ, indirect object and after prepositions#

Use the Dativ for the indirect object (the person who receives something) and after many common prepositions: mit, in, auf, an, bei, nach, seit, von, zu, aus, and others.

The Dativ has the most predictable rule of all three cases: after any article, the ending is almost always -en. Only without an article are there differences across genders.

Tip

A simple rule for Dativ: if there is any article before the adjective, use -en. Only without an article are the endings different: -em for masculine and neuter, -er for feminine, -en for plural.

Examples#

  • Ich wohne in einem kleinen Haus.
    I live in a small house. (neut, indefinite – -en)

  • Sie fährt mit dem alten Bus.
    She travels by the old bus. (masc, definite – -en)

  • Er hilft dem neuen Lehrer.
    He helps the new teacher. (masc, definite, indirect object – -en)

  • Wir treffen uns bei einer netten Freundin.
    We’re meeting at a nice friend’s place. (fem, indefinite – -en)

  • Mit frischem Brot schmeckt das Frühstück besser.
    With fresh bread, breakfast tastes better. (neut, no article – -em, not -en)

  • Sie trinkt den Kaffee mit warmer Milch.
    She drinks her coffee with warm milk. (fem, no article – -er)

Summary#

Key points

  • Adjective endings only apply when the adjective is directly before a noun, not after sein, werden, or bleiben

  • Three things control the ending: gender (masc/fem/neut/plural), case (Nom/Akk/Dat), and article type (definite / indefinite-possessive / no article)

  • Nominativ → Akkusativ: only masculine singular changes, all other genders and plural are identical across both cases

  • Dativ after any article: almost always -en; without an article: -em (masc/neut), -er (fem), -en (plural)

  • Genitive endings are a B1 topic, you do not need them yet

Common mistakes

  • ein neue Mann → ein neuer Mann (masculine Nominativ after indefinite article: -er, not -e)

  • mit dem alte Bus → mit dem alten Bus (Dativ after any article: always -en)

  • einen neuer Freund → einen neuen Freund (masculine Akkusativ: -en, not -er)

  • das altes Haus → das alte Haus (after a definite article, the ending is -e, never use strong endings like -es)

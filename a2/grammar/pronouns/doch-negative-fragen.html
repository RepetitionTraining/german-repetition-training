Doch bei Verneinungsfragen#

Using doch to answer negative questions and statements

See also

  • A2 > Grammar > Interrogativartikel: was für ein und welch-

  • A2 > Concepts > Indirekte Fragen

  • A2 > Concepts > Polite requests, wishes & advice

German has a special response word that English does not have: doch. You use it when someone asks a negative question or says a negative statement, but you want to say the positive opposite. Learning this early helps you avoid many misunderstandings in daily conversations.

Important

  • German has a three-way answer system: ja, nein, doch.

  • Use doch to contradict a negative question or statement.

  • After doch, continue with a positive sentence for clarity.

Das Dreiersystem: ja, nein, doch#

In many situations, German uses three short answer words:

  • ja: yes to a positive question

  • nein: no

  • doch: yes, but against a negative idea

Question type

If you agree positively

If you disagree / say no

Positive question

ja

nein

Negative question

doch (to correct)

nein (to confirm the negative)

Example Set: Three-way System#

  • Kommst du morgen? - Ja, ich komme morgen.
    Are you coming tomorrow? - Yes, I am coming tomorrow.

  • Kommst du morgen? - Nein, ich komme morgen nicht.
    Are you coming tomorrow? - No, I am not coming tomorrow.

  • Kommst du morgen nicht? - Doch, ich komme morgen.
    Aren’t you coming tomorrow? - Yes, I am coming tomorrow.

  • Hast du keinen Hunger? - Doch, ich habe Hunger.
    Aren’t you hungry? - Yes, I am hungry.

  • Ist das nicht dein Fahrrad? - Doch, das ist mein Fahrrad.
    Isn’t that your bike? - Yes, it is my bike.

  • Habt ihr heute keinen Unterricht? - Doch, wir haben heute Unterricht.
    Don’t you have class today? - Yes, we do have class today.

Tip

If the question is negative and your meaning is positive, doch is usually the safest answer.

Wann benutzt du doch?#

Use doch when the other person uses a negative form and you want to say the opposite (positive) meaning.

Common negative markers in these questions or statements:

  • nicht

  • kein / keine / keinen …

  • nie

  • niemand

Example Set: Negative Markers#

  • Hast du heute nicht Zeit? - Doch, ich habe heute Zeit.
    Don’t you have time today? - Yes, I do have time today.

  • Hast du kein Geld dabei? - Doch, ich habe Geld dabei.
    Don’t you have any money with you? - Yes, I do have money with me.

  • Gehst du nie schwimmen? - Doch, ich gehe im Sommer oft schwimmen.
    Do you never go swimming? - Yes, I do, often in summer.

  • Kennt dich niemand hier? - Doch, meine Nachbarin kennt mich.
    Does nobody know you here? - Yes, my neighbor knows me.

  • Du hast keine Geschwister, oder? - Doch, ich habe einen Bruder.
    You don’t have siblings, right? - Actually, I do, I have a brother.

  • Ihr seid heute nicht im Büro? - Doch, wir sind heute im Büro.
    You’re not in the office today? - Yes, we are in the office today.

Note

The negative marker can be in a question or in a statement. The logic stays the same: negative prompt + positive correction = doch.

Antwortmuster: Doch, ich#

A short Doch! can already be enough. But in real conversation, it is often clearer and friendlier to continue with a full positive sentence.

Negative prompt + Doch, + positive statement

Example Set: Pattern in Use#

  • Du kommst nicht mit. - Doch, ich komme mit.
    You’re not coming along. - Yes, I am coming along.

  • Du trinkst keinen Kaffee. - Doch, ich trinke Kaffee.
    You don’t drink coffee. - Actually, I do drink coffee.

  • Ihr habt nie Zeit. - Doch, wir haben heute Abend Zeit.
    You never have time. - Actually, we do have time this evening.

  • Das funktioniert nicht. - Doch, das funktioniert.
    That doesn’t work. - Yes, it does work.

  • Du verstehst mich nicht. - Doch, ich verstehe dich.
    You don’t understand me. - Yes, I do understand you.

  • Sie kennt niemanden hier. - Doch, sie kennt ihre Kollegin hier.
    She doesn’t know anyone here. - Actually, she knows her colleague here.

Warning

Do not keep the negation after doch if you want to correct it.

  • Wrong: Doch, ich habe kein Geld. (This agrees with the negative idea.)

  • Better: Doch, ich habe Geld.

Doch bei negativen Aussagen im Alltag#

You use doch not only for questions, but also to disagree with a negative statement. This is common when you correct assumptions or false impressions.

Example Set: Correcting Assumptions#

  • Das stimmt nicht. - Doch, das stimmt.
    That’s not true. - Yes, it is true.

  • Du bist nie pünktlich. - Doch, heute bin ich pünktlich.
    You’re never on time. - Actually, I am on time today.

  • Ihr habt keine Reservierung. - Doch, wir haben eine Reservierung auf Müller.
    You don’t have a reservation. - Actually, we do have a reservation under Müller.

  • Niemand hilft dir. - Doch, meine Schwester hilft mir.
    Nobody helps you. - Actually, my sister helps me.

  • Du hast das nicht erklärt. - Doch, ich habe es erklärt.
    You didn’t explain that. - Yes, I did explain it.

  • Er war nie in Berlin. - Doch, er war letztes Jahr in Berlin.
    He has never been to Berlin. - Actually, he was in Berlin last year.

Tip

In polite conversation, add softeners after doch:

  • Doch, eigentlich …

  • Doch, ich glaube …

  • Doch, soweit ich weiß …

This keeps your correction clear but less confrontational.

Note

The word doch also has other jobs in German (for example as a modal particle inside statements). This page focuses only on doch as a response word to negative questions and statements.

Summary#

Key points

  • German often uses three response words in this area: ja, nein, doch.

  • Use doch when you answer a negative question or correct a negative statement with a positive meaning.

  • Typical negative triggers are nicht, kein, nie, and niemand.

  • A clear pattern is: Doch, + positive sentence.

  • Doch helps you disagree and correct misunderstandings in everyday conversations.

Common mistakes

  • Using ja to contradict a negative question: Kommst du nicht? - Ja. -> safer: Doch, ich komme.

  • Keeping negation after doch by accident: Doch, ich habe kein Geld. -> Doch, ich habe Geld.

  • Using doch when you actually agree with the negative: Du kommst nicht? - Doch. (wrong meaning) -> use Nein, wenn du wirklich nicht kommst.

  • Answering only with one word in unclear contexts. Better: add a short full sentence after doch.

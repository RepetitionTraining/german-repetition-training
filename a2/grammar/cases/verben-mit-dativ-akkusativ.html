Verben mit Dativ und Akkusativ#

Verbs with two objects: who receives something and what is given

See also

  • A2 > Grammar > Dativ – Grundlagen

  • A2 > Grammar > Akkusativ – Grundlagen

Note

Before reading this article, make sure you are comfortable with:

  • A2 > Grammar > Dativ – Grundlagen

  • A2 > Grammar > Akkusativ – Grundlagen

These verbs help you say that someone gets, receives, or hears something. You use one object for the person (dative, Wem?) and one object for the thing (accusative, Was?).

Important

  • Basic pattern: subject + verb + dative person + accusative thing

  • With two noun phrases, dative normally comes before accusative

  • If the accusative object is a pronoun, it usually comes before the dative object

Examples:

  • I give the child the book.

  • I give it to the child.

  • I explain it to him.

Das Grundmuster: Person im Dativ, Sache im Akkusativ#

Many common verbs can take two objects at the same time. Ask Wem? for the dative person and Was? for the accusative thing.

Subject + verb + dative object (person, Wem?) + accusative object (thing, Was?)

Verb

Meaning

Mini pattern

geben

to give

jemandem etwas geben

schenken

to gift

jemandem etwas schenken

zeigen

to show

jemandem etwas zeigen

bringen

to bring

jemandem etwas bringen

schicken

to send

jemandem etwas schicken

erklären

to explain

jemandem etwas erklären

leihen

to lend

jemandem etwas leihen

anbieten

to offer

jemandem etwas anbieten

empfehlen

to recommend

jemandem etwas empfehlen

Examples:

  • Ich gebe meiner Schwester das Buch.
    I give my sister the book.

  • Wir schenken unserer Mutter Blumen.
    We give our mother flowers.

  • Der Lehrer zeigt den Schülern eine Karte.
    The teacher shows the students a map.

  • Kannst du mir deinen Stift leihen?
    Can you lend me your pen?

  • Ich biete dir einen Kaffee an.
    I’m offering you a coffee.

  • Sie empfiehlt ihrem Freund diesen Film.
    She recommends this film to her friend.

Tip

When you build a sentence, first find the person (Wem?), then the thing (Was?). This helps you pick the right case quickly.

Wortstellung: Nomen und Pronomen#

With two noun phrases, German usually puts the dative object before the accusative object. But pronouns are more “compact” and can move forward.

Situation

Typical order

Example

Dative noun + accusative noun

dative before accusative

Ich gebe dem Kind das Buch.

Accusative pronoun + dative noun

accusative pronoun first

Ich gebe es dem Kind.

Accusative pronoun + dative pronoun

accusative first, then dative

Ich gebe es ihm.

Examples:

  • Ich gebe dem Kind das Buch.
    I give the child the book.

  • Ich gebe es dem Kind.
    I give it to the child.

  • Ich gebe es ihm.
    I give it to him.

  • Kannst du es mir erklären?
    Can you explain it to me?

  • Der Kellner bringt dem Paar die Rechnung.
    The waiter brings the bill to the couple.

  • Der Kellner bringt sie ihnen.
    The waiter brings it to them.

Warning

A common mistake is to keep noun order when you replace the accusative noun with a pronoun. Not: Ich gebe dem Kind es. Better: Ich gebe es dem Kind.

Fragen mit Wem? und Was?#

You can ask for each object separately. Ask Wem? for the dative person and Was? for the accusative thing.

What you ask for

Question pattern

Example

Dative object (person)

Wem + verb + subject + accusative object?

Wem gibst du das Buch?

Accusative object (thing)

Was + verb + subject + dative object?

Was gibst du dem Kind?

Examples:

  • Wem gibst du das Buch?
    Who are you giving the book to?

  • Was gibst du dem Kind?
    What are you giving the child?

  • Wem schickst du die E-Mail?
    Who are you sending the email to?

  • Was schickst du deiner Freundin?
    What are you sending your friend?

  • Wem erklärt der Lehrer die Regel?
    Who is the teacher explaining the rule to?

  • Was erklärt der Lehrer den Kindern?
    What is the teacher explaining to the children?

Note

In answers, keep the same logic: dative answers Wem?, accusative answers Was?. This helps you check your own sentence.

Summary#

Key points

  • With verbs like geben, zeigen, schicken, you often need two objects: dative person (Wem?) and accusative thing (Was?).

  • Normal order with two noun phrases is usually dative before accusative: Ich gebe dem Kind das Buch.

  • If the accusative object is a pronoun, it moves before the dative object: Ich gebe es dem Kind.

  • If both objects are pronouns, use accusative first, then dative: Ich gebe es ihm.

  • Questions help you check case choice: Wem? for dative, Was? for accusative.

Common mistakes

  • Ich gebe dem Kind *es. → Ich gebe es dem Kind. (Accusative pronoun comes before the dative noun.)

  • Ich erkläre *ihm es. → Ich erkläre es ihm. (With two pronouns: accusative first, then dative.)

  • Wen gibst du dem Kind? → Was gibst du dem Kind? (Was? asks for the thing in accusative.)

  • Was gibst du *der Kind? → Was gibst du dem Kind? (Kind is neuter; dative singular article is dem.)

  • Ich empfehle *du den Film. → Ich empfehle dir den Film. (empfehlen needs a dative person.)

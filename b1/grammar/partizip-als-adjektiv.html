Partizip als Adjektiv#

Participles used as adjectives

German has two participle forms, the past participle (Partizip II) and the present participle (Partizip I), and both can work like regular adjectives to describe a noun. Instead of using a relative clause such as “the book that was ordered”, you can say “the ordered book” (das bestellte Buch). This makes your German more concise and natural-sounding.

When a participle is placed directly before a noun, it takes the same adjective endings as any other adjective. When it follows a linking verb like sein or bleiben, it stays unchanged with no ending.

Partizip II als Adjektiv#

The Partizip II is the same form you already know from the Perfekt tense. When used as an adjective, it tells you that something has been done to the noun, it describes the resulting state or condition.

Partizip II formation#

Verb type

How to form Partizip II

Example

Weak (regular)

ge- + stem + -t

kaufen → gekauft

Strong (irregular)

ge- + (new) stem + -en

schreiben → geschrieben

Inseparable prefix (ver-, be-, er-, ent-, …)

stem + -t or -en (no ge-)

bestellen → bestellt, vergessen → vergessen

-ieren verbs

stem + -t (no ge-)

reparieren → repariert

Separable prefix

prefix + ge- + stem + -t/-en

aufmachen → aufgemacht

Once you have the Partizip II, just add adjective endings to place it before a noun.

Partizip II before a noun#

  • Das gekaufte Buch liegt auf dem Tisch.
    The purchased book is on the table.

  • Ich esse gebratene Kartoffeln zum Abendessen.
    I’m eating fried potatoes for dinner.

  • Das reparierte Auto fährt jetzt wieder.
    The repaired car is running again now.

  • Das bestellte Essen ist noch nicht angekommen.
    The ordered food hasn’t arrived yet.

  • Die gestohlene Tasche wurde glücklicherweise gefunden.
    The stolen bag was luckily found.

  • Ein geöffnetes Fenster lässt frische Luft herein.
    An opened window lets fresh air in.

Tip

Verbs with inseparable prefixes (ver-, be-, er-, emp-, ent-, ge-, miss-) and verbs ending in -ieren do not get a ge- prefix in the Partizip II.

  • kaufen → gekauft, BUT bestellen → bestellt (be- is inseparable)

  • kochen → gekocht, BUT reparieren → repariert (-ieren verb)

Examples, predicative (after sein)#

When the participle follows sein or bleiben, it describes the current state of the subject. No adjective ending is used in this position.

  • Die Tür ist abgeschlossen.
    The door is locked.

  • Das Geschäft ist geschlossen.
    The shop is closed.

  • Mein Handy ist geladen.
    My phone is charged.

Partizip I als Adjektiv#

The Partizip I is formed by adding -d to the infinitive of any verb. It describes an action that is actively happening at the same moment, the noun is in the middle of doing something.

Partizip I formation#

Infinitive + -d → Partizip I

Partizip I

Meaning

weinen

weinend

crying

The formula works for all verbs without exception, just add -d to the infinitive and then the usual adjective endings.

Partizip I before a noun#

  • Das weinende Kind braucht Hilfe.
    The crying child needs help.

  • Der schlafende Hund liegt auf dem Sofa.
    The sleeping dog is lying on the sofa.

  • Die wartenden Gäste werden langsam ungeduldig.
    The waiting guests are slowly getting impatient.

  • Ein bellender Hund sitzt vor der Haustür.
    A barking dog is sitting in front of the front door.

  • Eine wachsende Stadt braucht neue Schulen.
    A growing city needs new schools.

  • Die spielenden Kinder sind laut und fröhlich.
    The playing children are loud and happy.

Note

The Partizip I can also appear after sein in a predicative position, but this is mostly found in written or literary language.

  • Er kam lachend zur Tür herein. – He came in through the door laughing.

For everyday spoken German, the Partizip I is mainly used before nouns. Notice it has no ge- prefix, this is one key way to tell it apart from a Partizip II.

Summary#

Key points

  • Partizip II describes a completed action or a resulting state, as in das bestellte Buch or die reparierte Uhr.

  • Partizip I describes an ongoing action, as in das schlafende Kind or der bellende Hund.

  • Before a noun, both participles take the same standard adjective endings as regular adjectives.

  • After sein or bleiben, the participle is used predicatively and does not take an adjective ending.

Common mistakes

  • Ein *weinend Kind → Ein weinendes Kind. (After ein, neuter nominative needs -es.)

  • Das *schlafendes Kind → Das schlafende Kind. (After definite das, the ending is -e, not -es.)

  • Das *geweinende Kind → Das weinende Kind. (Partizip I has no ge- prefix.)

  • Das *kaufte Buch → Das gekaufte Buch. (kaufte is Präteritum, not Partizip II.)

  • Das Fenster ist *geöffnetes → Das Fenster ist geöffnet. (Predicative use after sein takes no ending.)

  • Das *bestelltes Essen → Das bestellte Essen. (After definite das, the ending is -e, not -es.)

wegen und trotz#

“Because of” and “despite”, genitive prepositions

Two of the most useful prepositions in German are wegen (“because of”) and trotz (“despite”). Both take the genitive case in standard written German, which means the nouns and articles after them change form. Once you get the hang of the genitive endings, you can add more variety and precision to your writing and speech.

wegen + Genitiv#

wegen explains the reason or cause of something, it answers the question “why?” by pointing to a person, thing, or circumstance. It corresponds to “because of” or “due to” in English.

Structure:

wegen + genitive noun phrase (rest of sentence)

The genitive article forms for the noun after wegen:

Masculine

Feminine

Neuter

Plural

Definite article

des …-(e)s

der

des …-(e)s

der

Indefinite article

eines …-(e)s

einer

eines …-(e)s

–

Note

Masculines and neuters add -s (or -es for single-syllable nouns) to the noun itself: der Stau → des Staus, das Wetter → des Wetters, der Lärm → des Lärmes. Feminines and plurals do not change the noun.

Examples#

  • Wegen des Regens mussten wir den Ausflug absagen.
    Because of the rain, we had to cancel the trip.

  • Er kam zu spät, wegen des Staus auf der Autobahn.
    He arrived late because of the traffic jam on the motorway.

  • Wegen ihrer Erkältung konnte sie nicht zur Arbeit gehen.
    Because of her cold, she couldn’t go to work.

  • Wegen des lauten Lärms konnte ich kaum schlafen.
    Because of the loud noise, I could barely sleep.

  • Er gab seinen Job wegen der langen Arbeitszeiten auf.
    He quit his job because of the long working hours.

  • Die Veranstaltung wurde wegen eines technischen Problems verschoben.
    The event was postponed because of a technical problem.

Tip

When the wegen phrase comes at the start of the sentence, the subject and verb swap positions, the verb must remain in second place:

  • Wegen des Regens bleibe ich zu Hause. (not: Wegen des Regens ich bleibe …)

trotz + Genitiv#

trotz introduces a contradiction or concession, it says that something happens even though there is an obstacle or opposing factor. It corresponds to “despite” or “in spite of” in English.

Structure:

trotz + genitive noun phrase (rest of sentence)

The genitive article forms are the same as for wegen (see table above).

Examples#

  • Trotz des Regens gingen wir spazieren.
    Despite the rain, we went for a walk.

  • Sie kam trotz ihrer Müdigkeit zur Party.
    She came to the party despite her tiredness.

  • Trotz des hohen Preises haben wir die Karte gekauft.
    Despite the high price, we bought the ticket.

  • Wir haben das Projekt trotz aller Schwierigkeiten abgeschlossen.
    We finished the project despite all the difficulties.

  • Trotz der schlechten Nachrichten blieb sie optimistisch.
    Despite the bad news, she remained optimistic.

  • Er schaffte die Prüfung trotz seiner kurzen Vorbereitungszeit.
    He passed the exam despite his short preparation time.

Note

Do not confuse trotz + Genitiv with the adverb trotzdem (“nevertheless” / “anyway”). They are related in meaning but work very differently: Trotz des Regens … (preposition + noun phrase) vs. Es regnet. Ich gehe trotzdem. (adverb in the main clause).

wegen versus weil#

Both wegen and weil give a reason, but they work differently. wegen is a preposition followed by a noun phrase; weil is a subordinating conjunction that introduces a full clause with the verb at the end.

Structure

Example

wegen

preposition + genitive noun phrase

Ich bleibe zu Hause wegen meiner Erkältung.

weil

conjunction + subject + … + verb (end)

Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich krank bin.

Both sentences mean the same thing. Use wegen when you have a noun phrase; use weil when you have a full subject + verb clause.

Examples#

  • Ich bleibe zu Hause wegen meiner Erkältung.
    I’m staying home because of my cold.

  • Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich krank bin.
    I’m staying home because I’m sick.

  • Das Konzert wurde wegen eines Sturms abgesagt.
    The concert was cancelled because of a storm.

  • Das Konzert wurde abgesagt, weil ein Sturm drohte.
    The concert was cancelled because a storm was approaching.

  • Wegen des Streiks fahren keine Züge.
    Because of the strike, no trains are running.

  • Keine Züge fahren, weil die Mitarbeiter streiken.
    No trains are running because the employees are on strike.

Tip

In everyday speech, weil is more common. Use wegen when you want to keep things short and noun-based, for example in news headlines, formal emails, or official notices.

Spoken German: dative variant#

In colloquial speech, especially in southern Germany, Austria, and Switzerland, both wegen and trotz are very often followed by the dative rather than the genitive:

Spoken (dative)

Standard written (genitive)

wegen dem Regen

wegen des Regens

trotz dem Schnee

trotz des Schnees

wegen der Arbeit

wegen der Arbeit (same, feminine!)

Note

For feminine and plural nouns, the dative and genitive article forms happen to look the same (der in both cases), so the distinction only shows up clearly with masculine and neuter nouns. In formal writing and exams, always use the genitive.

Summary#

Key points

  • wegen + Genitiv = because of (gives a reason)

  • trotz + Genitiv = despite / in spite of (introduces a contrasting fact)

  • Masculine and neuter nouns add -(e)s in the genitive: des Regens, des Wetters

  • Adjectives after a definite or indefinite article in the genitive always end in -en: wegen des schlechten Wetters

  • In spoken German, dative is widely used instead: wegen dem Regen, trotz dem Schnee

Common mistakes

  • wegen dem Regen (in writing) → wegen des Regens (genitive in standard German; dative is only for speech)

  • trotz dem schlechten Wetter (in writing) → trotz des schlechten Wetters (genitive required)

  • wegen des schlechte Wetter → wegen des schlechten Wetters (adjective must end in -en after a genitive article)

  • wegen des Regen → wegen des Regens (masculine noun needs the -s ending in the genitive)

  • Es regnet, wegen des Regens → Wegen des Regens … (wegen is a preposition, not a connector between two clauses, use weil for a clause)

  • Wegen des Regens, ich bleibe zu Hause. → Wegen des Regens bleibe ich zu Hause. (verb stays in second position, no comma after the preposition phrase)

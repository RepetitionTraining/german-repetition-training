Plusquamperfekt (Past Perfect)#

German has three main past tenses that map to different points on the timeline. The Präsens describes now; the Perfekt and Präteritum describe earlier events; and the Plusquamperfekt steps back even further, to “the past before the past”. You use it to make clear that one thing had already happened when something else occurred.

Examples:

  • I had already eaten when she arrived.

  • He had left before the meeting started.

  • By the time we got there, the concert had already begun.

Forming the Plusquamperfekt#

The Plusquamperfekt is built from two parts: the Präteritum form of haben or sein, followed by the Partizip II of the main verb. Because you already know the Partizip II from the Perfekt, the only new element here is switching the auxiliary to its Präteritum form.

The Partizip II comes at the end of the sentence (or clause).

Examples

  • Ich hatte den Film schon gesehen, bevor er im Kino anlief.
    I had already seen the film before it was released in cinemas.

  • Er hatte die Wohnung aufgeräumt und wartete auf die Gäste.
    He had tidied the flat and was waiting for the guests.

  • Sie hatte das Formular ausgefüllt und abgegeben.
    She had filled in the form and handed it in.

  • Das Konzert hatte schon angefangen, als wir ankamen.
    The concert had already started when we arrived.

  • Wir hatten alles vorbereitet, aber das Wetter spielte nicht mit.
    We had prepared everything, but the weather didn’t cooperate.

  • Der Zug war schon abgefahren, als er den Bahnhof erreichte.
    The train had already departed when he reached the station.

  • Sie waren früh aufgestanden und hatten Zeit für ein ruhiges Frühstück.
    They had got up early and had time for a quiet breakfast.

Tip

Choose war + Partizip II for verbs that express movement from one place to another (fahren, gehen, kommen, ankommen, fliegen, reisen) or a change of state (aufwachen, einschlafen, aufstehen, werden). All other verbs take hatte. This is identical to the haben/sein rule you already know from the Perfekt.

Plusquamperfekt with “nachdem”#

One of the most important uses of the Plusquamperfekt is in temporal clauses introduced by “nachdem” (after). The rule is simple: the nachdem clause describes the earlier event, use the Plusquamperfekt there. The main clause describes what happened next, use the Präteritum or Perfekt there.

Nachdem + subject + … + Partizip II + hatte/war,
main clause in Präteritum or Perfekt.

Note

In a nachdem clause the auxiliary (hatte or war) is always the last word. The Partizip II comes just before it: …gegessen hatte, …angekommen war.

Examples

  • Nachdem er gefrühstückt hatte, fuhr er zur Arbeit.
    After he had had breakfast, he drove to work.

  • Nachdem wir die Rechnung bezahlt hatten, verließen wir das Restaurant.
    After we had paid the bill, we left the restaurant.

  • Nachdem das Kind eingeschlafen war, räumten die Eltern auf.
    After the child had fallen asleep, the parents tidied up.

  • Er rief an, nachdem er die E-Mail gelesen hatte.
    He called after he had read the email.

  • Nachdem sie umgezogen war, lernte sie viele neue Leute kennen.
    After she had moved (house), she got to know many new people.

  • Nachdem wir alles gepackt hatten, konnten wir endlich losfahren.
    After we had packed everything, we could finally set off.

Tip

The nachdem clause can come at the start or the end of the sentence. When it comes first, the main clause begins with the verb (V2 word order still applies): Nachdem er gegessen hatte, ging er spazieren. When it comes second, the word order is normal: Er ging spazieren, nachdem er gegessen hatte.

Summary#

Key points

  • Form: hatte or war (Präteritum of haben/sein) + Partizip II

  • Use hatte + Partizip II for most verbs; use war + Partizip II for movement and change-of-state verbs (same rule as in the Perfekt)

  • The Partizip II is identical to the one you already know from the Perfekt, nothing new to learn

  • After nachdem, always use the Plusquamperfekt; the auxiliary (hatte/war) goes to the very end of the clause

Common mistakes

  • Nachdem er *gegessen hat, ging er schlafen. → gegessen hatte (Plusquamperfekt, not Perfekt, is required after nachdem)

  • Er *hatte gegangen. → Er war gegangen. (movement verbs take war, not hatte)

  • Nachdem er hatte gegessen, … → Nachdem er gegessen hatte, … (the auxiliary goes to the very end of the clause)

  • Sie hatte das Buch *lesen. → Sie hatte das Buch gelesen. (Partizip II, not infinitive)

  • Das Kind war *geschlafen. → Das Kind hatte geschlafen. (schlafen is a state, not a movement or change, it takes hatte)

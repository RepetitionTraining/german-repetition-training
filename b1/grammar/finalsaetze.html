Finalsätze#

Purpose clauses: expressing goals and intentions with “um…zu” and “damit”

Finalsätze let you express the goal or intention behind an action, not why something happened (that is what weil is for), but what you want to achieve. In English, this sounds like “in order to”, “so that”, or simply “to”. German has two main constructions for this: um…zu and damit. Choosing between them comes down to one question: do both parts of the sentence share the same subject?

„um…zu” + Infinitiv#

Use um…zu when the subject performing the action and the subject pursuing the goal are the same person or thing. After um, there is no separate subject, the infinitive phrase simply names the purpose. The infinitive always goes at the very end of the phrase.

[Main clause], um + [other elements] + zu + Infinitiv

Examples:

  • Ich spare Geld, um eine neue Jacke zu kaufen.
    I’m saving money in order to buy a new jacket.

  • Lisa macht Yoga, um nach der Arbeit zu entspannen.
    Lisa does yoga in order to relax after work.

  • Wir nehmen den Zug, um schneller ans Ziel zu kommen.
    We’re taking the train in order to get to our destination faster.

  • Er trinkt jeden Morgen Kaffee, um wach zu werden.
    He drinks coffee every morning to wake up.

  • Ich rufe das Hotel an, um ein Zimmer zu reservieren.
    I’m calling the hotel to reserve a room.

  • Um pünktlich anzukommen, nehme ich den Frühzug.
    In order to arrive on time, I take the early train.

Tip

Separable verbs and “um…zu”: the prefix and “zu” merge into a single word, written together without a space:

  • aufmachen → um aufzumachen

  • anrufen → um anzurufen

  • einschlafen → um einzuschlafen

The “um…zu” phrase can also come first in the sentence. When it does, the main verb follows directly after the comma: Um fit zu bleiben, mache ich täglich Sport.

„damit” – Nebensatz#

Use damit when the subject performing the action and the person who benefits from or carries out the goal are different people or things. “damit” introduces a full subordinate clause with its own subject, and the conjugated verb goes to the very end, just like in any other Nebensatz.

[Main clause], damit + Subjekt + [other elements] + conjugated verb (at end)

Examples:

  • Ich erkläre es nochmal, damit du es verstehst.
    I’ll explain it again so that you understand.

  • Er spricht langsam, damit alle ihn gut hören können.
    He speaks slowly so that everyone can hear him.

  • Meine Eltern kaufen ein zweites Bett, damit Oma bei uns schlafen kann.
    My parents are buying a second bed so that grandma can sleep at our place.

  • Ich packe den Regenschirm ein, damit wir nicht nass werden.
    I’m packing the umbrella so that we don’t get wet.

  • Die Lehrerin schreibt die Wörter an die Tafel, damit die Schüler sie abschreiben können.
    The teacher writes the words on the board so that the students can copy them.

Note

“damit” with the same subject: It is grammatically possible to use “damit” even when the subjects are the same, for example: Ich lerne viel, damit ich die Prüfung bestehe. However, in this case “um…zu” is the more natural and concise choice. Aim to use “um…zu” whenever subjects are the same, it is a clear sign of fluency.

Same subject or different? Choosing the right construction#

The key question is always: who is the subject in each part of the sentence?

Situation

Use

Example

Same subject in both parts

um…zu

Ich lerne Deutsch, um besser sprechen zu können.

Different subjects

damit

Ich schreibe es auf, damit du es nicht vergisst.

Same subject, also valid

damit

Ich lerne Deutsch, damit ich besser sprechen kann.

Parallel examples, one context, two different subject situations:

  • Ich mache das Licht aus, um Strom zu sparen.
    I turn off the light to save electricity. (same subject: ich → ich)

  • Ich mache das Licht aus, damit du schlafen kannst.
    I turn off the light so that you can sleep. (different subjects: ich → du)

Tip

A quick mental test: translate the sentence mentally into English. After “so that”, is the subject the same person as before, or a different person?

  • Same person → um…zu

  • Different person → damit

Summary#

Key points

  • Same subject in both clauses → use um…zu (preferred, more natural)

  • Different subjects → you must use damit

  • With damit: full subordinate clause, the conjugated verb goes to the end

  • With um…zu: no subject after “um”, only an infinitive at the very end

  • Both answer: Wozu? / Mit welchem Ziel? (What for? / With what goal?)

Common mistakes

  • Ich lerne, um ich fit zu sein. → Ich lerne, um fit zu sein. (“um…zu” has no subject, drop the pronoun)

  • Er spart, damit kauft er ein Auto. → Er spart, damit er ein Auto kaufen kann. (verb goes to the end in damit-clauses)

  • Ich rufe dich an, damit du weißt Bescheid. → damit du Bescheid weißt. (the entire predicate goes to the end, not just the main verb)

  • um anzurufe → um anzurufen (use the infinitive, not a conjugated form)

  • um aufmachen → um aufzumachen (separable verbs need “zu” inserted between the prefix and the stem)

  • Ich lerne, um die Prüfung bestehe. → um die Prüfung zu bestehen (use “zu + Infinitiv”, not a conjugated verb)

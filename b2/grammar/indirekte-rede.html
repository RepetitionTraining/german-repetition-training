Indirekte Rede#

Report statements clearly and neutrally in formal and spoken German

See also

  • B2 > Grammar > Wortstellung im Satz

  • B2 > Grammar > Irreale Vergleichssätze

  • B1 > Grammar > Konjunktiv II

  • B1 > Grammar > Passiv

With indirekte Rede, you can report what other people say without presenting it as your own claim. At B2, this is especially important in formal contexts like scientific writing, newspaper articles, and news broadcasts. The usual signal is Konjunktiv I, but in everyday conversation you will also hear many Indikativ forms.

Examples:

  • The spokesperson says the plan is realistic.

  • The article reports that experts expect slower growth.

  • In daily conversation, people often report this in the normal indicative form.

Why German uses Konjunktiv I in reported speech#

In formal German, Konjunktiv I marks distance from the content: you show that you are repeating someone else’s words. This is why it appears so often in journalism, news language, and academic reporting.

Context

Typical choice

Effect

News report

Konjunktiv I

neutral reporting tone

Scientific text

Konjunktiv I

clear source distance

Everyday conversation

often Indikativ

more direct, less formal

Examples:

  • Die Ministerin erklärt, die Lage sei stabil.
    The minister explains that the situation is stable.

  • Die Zeitung berichtet, der Konzern plane weitere Investitionen.
    The newspaper reports that the corporation plans further investments.

  • Im Interview sagt der Forscher, die Daten zeigten einen klaren Trend.
    In the interview, the researcher says the data show a clear trend.

  • Der Nachrichtensprecher meldet, mehrere Flüge fielen aus.
    The news anchor reports that several flights were canceled.

Tip

If you want a neutral, professional tone, choose Konjunktiv I first. It signals: “I am reporting, not confirming.”

Building indirect speech: forms and sentence patterns#

You can report statements with dass clauses or without dass. Without dass, the reported clause often stays in main-clause word order, but the verb appears in Konjunktiv I.

Type

Pattern

Example

with dass

reporting verb + dass + … + Konjunktiv I at the end

Er sagt, dass er morgen komme.

without dass

reporting verb + comma + Konjunktiv I clause

Er sagt, er komme morgen.

yes/no question

reporting verb + ob + … + Verb (usually Konjunktiv) at the end

Sie fragt, ob er schon angekommen sei.

Examples:

  • Der Sprecher sagt, dass die Verhandlungen morgen beginnen.
    The spokesperson says that the negotiations begin tomorrow.

  • Der Sprecher sagt, die Verhandlungen beginnen morgen.
    The spokesperson says the negotiations begin tomorrow.

  • Sie fragte, ob die Lieferung schon angekommen sei.
    She asked whether the delivery had already arrived.

  • Er wollte wissen, warum der Termin verschoben worden sei.
    He wanted to know why the appointment had been postponed.

Note

At B2, both patterns (with and without dass) are useful. The version without dass is common in dense written reporting, especially when several reported clauses follow each other.

When Konjunktiv I and Indikativ look identical#

Some Konjunktiv I forms are identical to Indikativ (especially with ich, wir, and often sie plural). In those cases, German often switches to Konjunktiv II (or a würde-form) so the reported-speech signal stays clear.

Situation

Preferred strategy

Example

K I = Indikativ

use Konjunktiv II

Er sagt, sie hätten keine Zeit.

K II also unclear/stiff

use würde + Infinitiv

Er sagt, sie würden später kommen.

Examples:

  • Die Zeugin sagte, sie hätten den Fahrer nicht erkannt.
    The witness said they had not recognized the driver.

  • Der Analyst schrieb, die Märkte würden kurzfristig nervös bleiben.
    The analyst wrote that the markets would remain volatile in the short term.

  • Er sagt, wir wären bereit für den nächsten Schritt.
    He says we would be ready for the next step.

  • Die Redaktion meldet, die Verhandlungen würden heute fortgesetzt.
    The editorial team reports that the negotiations would continue today.

Warning

Do not force Konjunktiv I if it sounds identical to Indikativ and creates ambiguity. At B2, clarity is more important than mechanical form choice.

Spoken German: when Indikativ is natural#

In everyday spoken German, many speakers report statements in Indikativ, especially with dass. This is normal in conversation and informal settings. At B2, you should understand both systems and choose according to register.

Register

Typical form

Example

formal written (news/science)

mostly Konjunktiv I

Er sagte, die Methode sei zuverlässig.

informal spoken

often Indikativ

Er hat gesagt, dass die Methode zuverlässig ist.

Examples:

  • Formal: Der Autor schreibt, das Ergebnis sei reproduzierbar.
    Formal: The author writes that the result is reproducible.

  • Informal: Er hat gesagt, dass das Ergebnis reproduzierbar ist.
    Informal: He said that the result is reproducible.

  • Formal: Die Sprecherin erklärt, die Situation habe sich verbessert.
    Formal: The spokesperson explains that the situation has improved.

  • Informal: Sie meint, die Situation hat sich verbessert.
    Informal: She thinks the situation has improved.

Tip

In exams and formal writing, prefer Konjunktiv-based indirect speech. In real conversation, an Indikativ report with dass is often the most natural choice.

Summary#

Key points

  • In indirekte Rede, German typically uses Konjunktiv I to show you are reporting someone else’s words.

  • This is especially common in scientific writing, newspaper articles, and news broadcasts.

  • You can report with dass clauses or without dass; both are standard.

  • If Konjunktiv I is identical to Indikativ, use Konjunktiv II or a würde + Infinitiv form.

  • In spoken everyday German, Indikativ is very common and usually not considered wrong in informal contexts.

Common mistakes

  • Er sagt, er ist krank. (formal report style) -> Er sagt, er sei krank. (In formal reporting, Konjunktiv marks indirect speech.)

  • Sie fragte, ob er kommt. (formal report style) -> Sie fragte, ob er komme. (In formal style, questions in indirect speech are usually in Konjunktiv.)

  • Die Redaktion schreibt, wir kommen später. -> Die Redaktion schreibt, wir kämen später / wir würden später kommen. (Use a fallback when Konjunktiv I and Indikativ are identical.)

  • Er sagte, dass die Daten sei korrekt. -> Er sagte, dass die Daten korrekt seien. (Subject-verb agreement still applies in indirect speech.)

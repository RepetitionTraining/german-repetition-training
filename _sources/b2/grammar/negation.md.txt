At B2, negation is not only about saying no. You also learn how German builds negative meaning inside words and how the exact position of **nicht** changes focus. This helps you sound precise in discussions, reports, and nuanced everyday communication.

*Examples:*

- *The proposal is not impossible, but it is unrealistic right now.*
- *I am not saying that the plan is wrong, only that it is incomplete.*
- *This is a non-issue for us, but a major issue for the client.*

## Lexical negation with des-, dis-, miss-

These prefixes often appear in formal or academic vocabulary. They can express absence, reversal, or a wrong/undesirable version of an action or state.

| Prefix | Typical effect | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **des-** | lack/removal of orientation or interest | desorientiert, desinteressiert |
| **dis-** | separation, mismatch, loss of validity | disqualifizieren, diskontinuierlich |
| **miss-** | wrong execution or harmful use | missverstehen, missbrauchen |

**Examples:**

- Nach dem Nachtflug war ich völlig **des**orientiert.  
  *After the night flight, I was completely disoriented.*
- Er wirkte bei dem Thema auffällig **des**interessiert.  
  *He seemed noticeably disinterested in the topic.*
- Das Team wurde wegen eines Regelverstoßes **dis**qualifiziert.  
  *The team was disqualified due to a rule violation.*
- Die Entwicklung war in den letzten Monaten **dis**kontinuierlich.  
  *Development was discontinuous over the last months.*
- Ich habe deine Nachricht völlig **miss**verstanden.  
  *I completely misunderstood your message.*
- Der **Miss**brauch persönlicher Daten bleibt ein großes Problem.  
  *The misuse of personal data remains a major problem.*

:::{warning}
Not every word with these letter sequences is a transparent negation pattern. Learn frequent full words as chunks, especially in formal registers.
:::

## Lexical negation with un-, in-, il-, ir-, a-, non-

These forms are very common at B2 because they appear in abstract adjectives and nouns. Some are native German patterns (**un-**), while others are mostly used with international/Latinate vocabulary (**in-, il-, ir-, a-, non-**).

| Prefix | Typical use | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **un-** | very productive with everyday adjectives/nouns | unfair, unklar, Unsicherheit |
| **in- / il- / ir-** | mainly with international adjectives | inaktiv, illegal, irrelevant |
| **a-** | mostly in learned/technical words | asymmetrisch, atypisch |
| **non-** | explicit classificatory negation | nonverbal, Nonstopflug |

**Examples:**

- Die neue Regel ist für viele Mitarbeitende **un**klar.  
  *The new rule is unclear for many employees.*
- Seine Reaktion war überraschend **un**emotional.  
  *His reaction was surprisingly unemotional.*
- Das alte System ist inzwischen **in**effizient.  
  *The old system is inefficient by now.*
- Eine solche Klausel wäre wahrscheinlich **il**legal.  
  *Such a clause would probably be illegal.*
- Dieser Einwand ist für die aktuelle Frage **ir**relevant.  
  *This objection is irrelevant to the current question.*
- Der Bericht beschreibt ein **a**symmetrisches Machtverhältnis.  
  *The report describes an asymmetric power relationship.*
- Ihre Antwort blieb bewusst **non**verbal.  
  *Her response remained deliberately nonverbal.*

:::{tip}
When several prefixes seem possible, choose the form that is actually established in usage. For example, German normally uses **illegal**, not *unlegal*.
:::

## Suffixes and compounds: -los, -frei, -leer, Nicht-

German also creates negative meaning through suffixes and compounds. This is very useful for compact, precise expression.

| Pattern | Core meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **-los** | without, lacking | arbeitslos, sprachlos |
| **-frei** | free from, without (often neutral/positive) | zuckerfrei, alkoholfrei |
| **-leer** | empty of, lacking content/substance | sinnleer, inhaltsleer |
| **Nicht- + Nomen** | explicit category negation | Nichtmitglied, Nichtzahlung |

**Examples:**

- Nach der Kündigung war er mehrere Monate arbeits**los**.  
  *After the dismissal, he was unemployed for several months.*
- Bei der Nachricht war sie kurz sprach**los**.  
  *She was briefly speechless at the news.*
- Ich trinke heute nur alkoholfrei**e** Getränke.  
  *I am only drinking alcohol-free beverages today.*
- Die Firma bietet jetzt auch zucker**frei**e Produkte an.  
  *The company now also offers sugar-free products.*
- Viele fanden die Debatte am Ende sinn**leer**.  
  *Many found the debate empty of meaning in the end.*
- Der Bericht blieb leider inhalts**leer** und vage.  
  *The report remained empty of content and vague.*
- Für **Nicht**mitglieder ist die Teilnahme teurer.  
  *Participation is more expensive for non-members.*
- Die lange **Nicht**zahlung führte zu einer Mahnung.  
  *The prolonged non-payment led to a reminder notice.*

:::{note}
You may see older outline notes that write **-ller**. In current standard usage, the common productive pattern here is **-leer** (for example, *sinnleer*, *inhaltsleer*).
:::

## Position of nicht in the sentence

At B2, you need to control what exactly is negated. A practical core rule is: **nicht** usually comes directly before the element you want to negate. In neutral sentence negation, it often appears late in the clause, before the verbal end-field.

| Negation target | Typical placement | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole statement (neutral) | late in the clause | Ich habe den Bericht heute **nicht** gelesen. |
| Specific element (focus) | right before the element | Ich habe **nicht** den Bericht gelesen, sondern die E-Mail. |
| Adjective/adverb | before adjective/adverb | Das ist **nicht** sinnvoll. |
| Prepositional phrase | before the phrase | Wir treffen uns **nicht** im Büro. |

**Examples:**

- Ich kann heute leider **nicht** kommen.  
  *Unfortunately, I cannot come today.*
- Ich komme **nicht** heute, sondern erst morgen.  
  *I am not coming today, but only tomorrow.*
- Sie hat den Vertrag **nicht** unterschrieben.  
  *She did not sign the contract.*
- Wir haben **nicht** über das Budget gesprochen, sondern über Personalfragen.  
  *We did not talk about the budget, but about personnel issues.*
- Das ist **nicht** meine endgültige Entscheidung.  
  *That is not my final decision.*
- Er reagierte **nicht** ruhig, sondern sehr nervös.  
  *He did not react calmly, but very nervously.*
- Die Gäste sind **nicht** mit dem Zug angereist.  
  *The guests did not arrive by train.*

:::{tip}
If you are unsure, test the sentence as a subordinate clause with **dass**. The position of **nicht** and the scope often become easier to see.
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- B2 negation includes both **word-level negation** (prefixes/suffixes/compounds) and **sentence-level negation** with **nicht**.
- Common lexical patterns are **des-/dis-/miss-**, **un-/in-/il-/ir-/a-/non-**, as well as **-los**, **-frei**, **-leer**, and **Nicht- + Nomen**.
- The position of **nicht** controls scope: it usually stands before the element being negated.
- In neutral sentence negation, **nicht** is often placed late in the clause before the verbal end-field.
- At B2, choosing between near-synonymous forms (for example **-los** vs **-frei**) is often a register and nuance decision.

:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *Die Klausel ist \*unlegal.* -> Die Klausel ist **illegal**. (Use established lexical forms, not invented combinations.)
- *Ich habe den Bericht gelesen \*nicht.* -> Ich habe den Bericht **nicht** gelesen. (nicht does not go after the final verbal element in standard usage.)
- *Sie kommt \*nicht morgen, sie kommt heute.* -> Sie kommt **nicht heute, sondern morgen**. (Place nicht directly before the focused element you contrast.)
- *Das ist ein \*Nicht Mitglied.* -> Das ist ein **Nichtmitglied**. (Nicht- compounds are written as one word.)
- *Die Debatte war \*inhaltsfrei.* -> Die Debatte war **inhaltsleer**. (Use lexicalized forms that match meaning and register.)

:::

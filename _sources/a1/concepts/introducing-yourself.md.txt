# Introducing yourself

After this page you can say who you are: your name, where you come from, where you live, and how old you are. You can also ask someone else the same questions, both formally (*Sie*) and informally (*du*).

## When to use it

- Meeting someone for the first time (course, work, neighbours)
- Checking in somewhere and giving your details
- Answering the most common first questions people ask a learner

## Key phrases

| German | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ich heiße … | My name is … | most common opener |
| Mein Name ist … | My name is … | slightly more formal |
| Ich komme aus … | I come from … | country or city |
| Ich wohne in … | I live in … | city or district |
| Ich bin … Jahre alt. | I am … years old. | with a number |
| Wie heißen Sie? / Wie heißt du? | What is your name? | formal / informal |
| Woher kommen Sie? / Woher kommst du? | Where are you from? | formal / informal |
| Freut mich! | Nice to meet you! | fixed reply |

## Saying who you are

Three verbs do almost all the work: *heißen* (to be called), *kommen* (to come), and *wohnen* (to live). With *ich* they all end in **-e**.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich heiße Anna Schmidt.
: My name is Anna Schmidt.

Ich komme aus Spanien.
: I come from Spain.

Ich wohne in Hamburg.
: I live in Hamburg.

Ich bin 25 Jahre alt.
: I am 25 years old.
:::

## Asking the questions back

A conversation goes both ways. The question words are *wie* (how/what), *woher* (from where), and *wo* (where). Choose *Sie* or *du* and keep it consistent.

| Formal (Sie) | Informal (du) | English |
|---|---|---|
| Wie heißen Sie? | Wie heißt du? | What's your name? |
| Woher kommen Sie? | Woher kommst du? | Where are you from? |
| Wo wohnen Sie? | Wo wohnst du? | Where do you live? |
| Wie alt sind Sie? | Wie alt bist du? | How old are you? |

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Wie heißen Sie? – Ich heiße Müller.
: What is your name? My name is Müller.

Woher kommst du? – Ich komme aus der Türkei.
: Where are you from? I'm from Turkey.

Wo wohnst du? – Ich wohne in Berlin.
: Where do you live? I live in Berlin.

Und du?
: And you? (the easiest way to return any question)
:::

:::{note}
Use **Sie** with adults you don't know, at offices, and in shops. Use **du** with friends, family, children, and usually between students in a course. When unsure, start with *Sie*.
:::

## Register and tone

Germans usually give surnames in formal settings (*Ich heiße Weber*) and first names in informal ones. *Freut mich!* is the safe, friendly response to any introduction. A handshake is common in formal contexts.

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- Four sentences carry a full self-introduction: *Ich heiße … / Ich komme aus … / Ich wohne in … / Ich bin … Jahre alt.*
- Return questions with *wie*, *woher*, and *wo*, or simply *Und du? / Und Sie?*
- Pick *Sie* or *du* and stay consistent. Default to *Sie* with strangers.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich bin heiße Anna.* → Ich **heiße** Anna. (heißen is the verb, don't add bin)
- *\*Ich habe 25 Jahre alt.* → Ich **bin** 25 Jahre alt. (age uses sein, not haben)
- *\*Wo kommst du?* → **Woher** kommst du? (origin asks "from where")
:::

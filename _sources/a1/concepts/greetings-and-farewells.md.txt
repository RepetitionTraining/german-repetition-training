# Greetings and farewells

After this page you can open and close any everyday encounter in German: greet someone at the right time of day, choose between formal and informal, and leave with a natural goodbye instead of an abrupt exit.

## When to use it

- Walking into a shop, office, or classroom
- Meeting neighbours, colleagues, or friends
- Ending a conversation, a phone call, or a visit

## Key phrases

| German | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hallo! | Hello! | informal, any time |
| Guten Morgen! | Good morning! | until about 10-11 |
| Guten Tag! | Good day! | the neutral daytime greeting |
| Guten Abend! | Good evening! | from about 18:00 |
| Gute Nacht! | Good night! | only when someone goes to bed |
| Tschüss! | Bye! | informal, very common |
| Auf Wiedersehen! | Goodbye! | formal |
| Bis bald! / Bis morgen! | See you soon! / See you tomorrow! | friendly sign-offs |

## Saying hello

*Hallo* works with anyone you would call *du*. In formal settings, pick the greeting that matches the time of day. Adding the person's name makes any greeting warmer.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Guten Morgen, Frau Weber.
: Good morning, Mrs Weber.

Guten Tag, Herr Schmidt.
: Good day, Mr Schmidt.

Hallo Anna, wie geht es dir?
: Hello Anna, how are you?
:::

A short *Wie geht's?* (how are you?) often follows the greeting. The standard reply is *Danke, gut* and, if you like, the question back: *Und dir? Und Ihnen?*

## Saying goodbye

*Tschüss* is the everyday farewell; *Auf Wiedersehen* is its formal counterpart. The *bis* phrases promise a next time, and a good wish on top is common and always well received.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Tschüss, bis morgen!
: Bye, see you tomorrow!

Schönen Tag noch!
: Have a nice day!

Danke, gleichfalls!
: Thanks, same to you!
:::

:::{note}
Regional greetings are everywhere in Germany: *Moin* in the north, *Grüß Gott* and *Servus* in the south. You do not need to use them, but recognising them helps.
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- *Hallo* and *Tschüss* cover informal hello and goodbye.
- Formal: *Guten Morgen / Guten Tag / Guten Abend* to greet, *Auf Wiedersehen* to leave.
- *Gute Nacht* is only for bedtime, not for leaving in the evening.
- *Bis bald, bis morgen, bis später* promise a next meeting.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Gute Nacht!* when leaving a restaurant → **Guten Abend!** or **Auf Wiedersehen!** (Gute Nacht is for going to bed)
- *\*Guten Morgen* at 15:00 → **Guten Tag.** (Morgen ends mid-morning)
- Leaving without a farewell: a quick **Tschüss!** is expected even after small transactions.
:::

# Noun Gender and Plurals

Every German noun carries two pieces of information that English nouns do not: a gender and a plural form of its own. Neither follows one neat rule, so the habit that pays off is learning each noun as a package: article, noun, plural. This page shows you what to look for.

## Every noun has a gender

German sorts all nouns into three genders: masculine (*der*), feminine (*die*), and neuter (*das*). The gender belongs to the word, not to the thing: *der Tisch* (table) is masculine, *die Tür* (door) is feminine, *das Fenster* (window) is neuter.

A few endings are reliable clues:

| Ending | Gender | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -ung | always *die* | die Wohnung |
| -chen | always *das* | das Mädchen |
| -in (person) | *die* | die Lehrerin |

For everything else, the article has to be learned with the noun. *Der Tisch*, never just *Tisch*.

## Nouns are always capitalised

German capitalises every noun, everywhere in the sentence, not only names. This is useful when reading: any capitalised word in the middle of a sentence is a noun.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Der {noun}`Mann` kauft {noun}`Brot` und {noun}`Käse`.
: The man buys bread and cheese.

Meine {noun}`Schwester` hat eine {noun}`Katze`.
: My sister has a cat.
:::

## The plural patterns

The plural article is always **die**. The noun itself changes in one of a handful of ways. You cannot predict the pattern reliably at A1, but knowing the five common ones makes plurals feel familiar instead of random.

| Pattern | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| -n / -en | die Frau | die Frauen |
| -e (often with umlaut) | der Tisch, der Stuhl | die Tische, die Stühle |
| -er (often with umlaut) | das Kind, das Buch | die Kinder, die Bücher |
| -s | das Auto | die Autos |
| no ending (often with umlaut) | das Zimmer, der Apfel | die Zimmer, die Äpfel |

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Der Apfel ist rot. Die **Äpfel** sind rot.
: The apple is red. The apples are red.

Das Kind spielt. Die **Kinder** spielen.
: The child is playing. The children are playing.

Die Wohnung ist groß. Die **Wohnungen** sind groß.
: The flat is big. The flats are big.
:::

:::{tip}
Learn nouns as a trio: *der Tisch, die Tische*. Dictionaries and vocabulary lists always show the plural; take it along from the start and you never have to guess later.
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- Three genders: *der*, *die*, *das*. Gender is part of the word; learn it with the noun.
- Endings -ung and -chen are reliable: always *die Wohnung*, always *das Mädchen*.
- Every noun is capitalised, everywhere in the sentence.
- The plural article is always *die*; the noun changes by pattern (-n/-en, -e, -er, -s, umlaut).
- Learn each noun as a trio: article, noun, plural.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich kaufe brot.* → Ich kaufe **Brot**. (nouns are always capitalised)
- *\*Die Kind spielt.* → **Das** Kind spielt. (gender belongs to the word; Kind is neuter)
- *\*Das Kinder spielen.* → **Die** Kinder spielen. (the plural article is always die)
:::

# The Dative Case

At A1 you meet the dative in one main place: after a small group of prepositions that always demand it. You do not need the full dative system yet; you need to recognise the forms and use them after these prepositions. That covers almost every dative you will hear at this level.

## Seven prepositions that always take the dative

*aus, bei, mit, nach, von, zu, seit*: after these seven, the dative follows automatically. No decision to make, no exceptions.

| Preposition | Core meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| aus | out of, from | aus der Schweiz |
| bei | at, with (a person/place) | bei meinen Eltern |
| mit | with, by (transport) | mit dem Bus |
| nach | after, to (places) | nach der Arbeit |
| von | from, of | von meiner Oma |
| zu | to (people, places) | zu dem Arzt |
| seit | since, for (time) | seit einem Jahr |

## The article forms

After these prepositions the articles change like this:

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Definite | dem | der | dem | den |
| Indefinite | einem | einer | einem | – |

*Kein* and the possessives take the same endings: *keinem, keiner, keinem, keinen*; *meinem, meiner, meinem, meinen*. In the dative plural the noun itself also adds **-n**: *bei meinen Elter**n***.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Ich fahre mit {dativ}`dem Bus`.
: I go by bus.

Sie kommt aus {dativ}`der Türkei`.
: She comes from Turkey.

Ich lerne seit {dativ}`einem Jahr` Deutsch.
: I have been learning German for a year.
:::

:::{tip}
*Zu dem* usually shortens to **zum** and *zu der* to **zur**: *zum Arzt*, *zur Schule*. These contractions get their own page later: [Contracted Prepositions](/a1/grammar/prepositions/kurzformen.md).
:::

## Everyday chunks

A few fixed phrases use dative pronouns long before you study them as a system. Learn these as whole chunks:

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Wie geht es {dativ}`dir`?
: How are you?

{dativ}`Mir` geht es gut.
: I am fine.

Wie geht es {dativ}`Ihnen`?
: How are you? (formal)
:::

## A look ahead

German also uses the dative for the indirect object, the receiver in sentences like *Ich gebe **dem Mann** das Buch* (I give the man the book). That system is A2 material; you will meet it properly in the A2 dative article. For now, the prepositions and chunks above are all you need.

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- Seven prepositions always take the dative: *aus, bei, mit, nach, von, zu, seit*.
- The definite articles become *dem* (m/n), *der* (f), *den* (plural).
- Dative plural nouns add -n: *bei meinen Eltern*.
- Learn *Wie geht es dir? Mir geht es gut.* as fixed chunks.
- The dative as indirect object comes at A2.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich fahre mit den Bus.* → Ich fahre mit **dem** Bus. (*mit* takes the dative)
- *\*Sie kommt aus die Schweiz.* → Sie kommt aus **der** Schweiz. (feminine dative is *der*)
- *\*mit die Kinder* → mit **den Kindern**. (dative plural: *den* plus -n on the noun)
:::

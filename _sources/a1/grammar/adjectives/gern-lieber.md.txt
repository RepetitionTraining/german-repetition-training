# gern, lieber, am liebsten

*Gern* says you like doing something. Its two bigger siblings rank your likes: *lieber* (prefer) and *am liebsten* (most of all). Three words, one ladder, and suddenly you can compare everything you enjoy.

## The ladder

| Step | Word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| like | gern | Ich trinke gern Kaffee. |
| prefer | lieber | Ich trinke lieber Tee. |
| like most | am liebsten | Am liebsten trinke ich Wasser. |

All three behave like *gern*: they follow the verb and change nothing else in the sentence.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Er isst **gern** Pizza.
: He likes eating pizza.

Sie isst **lieber** Salat.
: She prefers salad.

**Am liebsten** esse ich Schokolade.
: Most of all I like eating chocolate.
:::

## Comparing with *lieber*

*Lieber* picks one option over another, and the question with *oder* invites the choice.

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
Trinkst du **lieber** Kaffee oder Tee?
: Do you prefer coffee or tea?

Ich fahre **lieber** mit dem Zug.
: I prefer going by train.

Ich bleibe heute **lieber** zu Hause.
: I'd rather stay home today.
:::

## Fronting *am liebsten*

*Am liebsten* loves the first position; the verb stays second, as always: *Am liebsten höre ich Musik.*

**Examples:**

:::{div} ol-examples
**Am liebsten** höre ich Musik.
: Most of all I like listening to music.

Was isst du **am liebsten**?
: What do you like eating most?
:::

## Summary

:::{admonition} Key points
:class: note

- The ladder: *gern* → *lieber* → *am liebsten*.
- All three sit after the verb and leave the sentence untouched.
- *lieber ... oder ...?* asks for a preference.
- Fronting *am liebsten* is natural; the verb stays second.
:::

:::{admonition} Common mistakes
:class: caution

- *\*Ich mag lieber Tee trinken.* → Ich trinke **lieber** Tee. (lieber pairs with the action verb)
- *\*am liebstens* → am **liebsten**. (no extra s)
- *\*gerner* → **lieber**. (gern has no -er form; lieber IS the comparison)
:::
